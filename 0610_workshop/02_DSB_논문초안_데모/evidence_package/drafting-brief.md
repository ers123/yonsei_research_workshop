## Research Question

- Primary RQ: How does a screen-time and physical-activity-linked visual bar interface shape children's perceived behavioral control and self-regulation of smartphone use?
- Secondary RQs: Does the design-led intervention reduce screen time and increase physical activity? Which design elements appear to support planning, feedback, incentive response, or parent-child negotiation? Under what conditions does the intervention remain external control rather than internalized self-regulation?
- Tentative thesis or argument: A persistent visual signifier that links physical activity to screen-time availability can make behavioral consequences legible enough to support planning and perceived control, but incentive and penalty mechanisms may also instrumentalize exercise and require careful interpretation through motivation and autonomy frameworks.
- Claims that must not be made: Do not claim clinical efficacy, long-term habit formation, universal screen-time reduction, validated causal effects, publication readiness, or child-level behavioral diagnosis without named human/statistical review.
