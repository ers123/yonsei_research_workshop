## Likely Reviewer Objections

| Risk | Why a reviewer may object | Required upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Literature review too thin | Current draft uses supplied pilot work and Ajzen theory but lacks a full digital wellbeing, HCI, child screen-time, and behavior-change design review | Add venue-specific literature review with verified citations |
| Methods underspecified | Recruitment, participant demographics, intervention procedure, consent/IRB status, inclusion/exclusion, and exact intervention duration are not yet fully documented in the manuscript | Add a full methods table and procedural timeline after human confirmation |
| Quantitative claims not ready | Dataset is described, but cleaning rules and statistical model are not finalized | Produce data-cleaning memo, analysis plan, and statistical output tables |
| Qualitative evidence not quotation-ready | Themes are plausible, but direct excerpts and coding reliability are absent | Prepare anonymized quotation table, coding scheme, and bilingual translation notes |
| Contribution may read as speculative | The manuscript argues for legibility and perceived control, but construct measurement is not yet established | Separate design-intent, observed interpretation, and measured psychological constructs |
| Pilot/current-study boundary | Pilot article can be mistaken as evidence for the current dataset | Explicitly label pilot evidence as design lineage, not current-study proof |
| Translation-sensitive terms | "Bar", "signifier", "perceived behavioral control", "self-regulation", and "instrumentalization" may be interpreted differently in Korean and English | Approve a bilingual glossary before external circulation |
| Ethics of incentive design | Linking exercise to screen-time access may be criticized as instrumentalizing physical activity | Treat this as a core discussion contribution and add human ethics review |
